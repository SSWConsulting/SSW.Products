YakShaver usually turns your recording into a work item (e.g. a PBI or bug). It can also [send an email](#example-2-asking-yakshaver-to-send-an-email) instead.

Here are some tips to create the perfect work item (or email):

## Work item or email?

### Example 1: Recording for a PBI

Say which project it is for and what you want changed. YakShaver creates a work item in that project.

> “This is for the YakShaver project. On yakshaver.ai, when I go to the Docs page and open the search box, the console shows two accessibility errors: the dialog has no title and no description. Screen reader users won’t know what the dialog is. Please CC Calum.”

![](/YakShaver/Docs/good-pbi-example.png)

**✅ Figure: Good example - A PBI created by YakShaver from a 54-second recording, with a clear title, Pain, Reproduce Steps, Acceptance Criteria and a screenshot**

### Example 2: Asking YakShaver to send an email

Whether you get an email depends on how you record:

| | [Microsoft Teams or the browser extension](#microsoft-teams-or-the-browser-extension) | [YakShaver Anywhere](#yakshaver-anywhere) |
|---|---|---|
| **What makes it an email** | Asking for one, e.g. *"please send an email to Tino"* | The project's work item destination is **Email** |
| **Who gets it** | The one person you name | Everyone you name |
| **CC** | Say *"CC ..."* | Say *"CC ..."*, and you are CC'd automatically |
| **If no name matches** | Teams sends you a card to pick the recipient | The email is sent to you |

#### Microsoft Teams or the browser extension

When you record with [Microsoft Teams](/docs/recording-work-item-teams) or the [browser extension](/docs/recording-work-item-browser-extension), just ask for an email - say *"please send an email to Tino"* or *"can you email this to Tino?"*. Mentioning an email is not enough; it has to be a request to send one. As with any request, [state your intent](#section-1-state-your-intent) clearly.

#### YakShaver Anywhere

In YakShaver Anywhere, the project decides. An admin sets a project's **work item destination** to **Email** - the same setting used to send shaves to [Azure DevOps](/docs/connect-azure-devops-work-items) or [Jira Cloud](/docs/connect-jira-cloud). A project with any other destination creates a work item, even if you say "send an email".

In an Email project, say who the email is for. YakShaver matches the names you say (or their [nicknames](/docs/assign-using-nicknames)) against people in your organization who use YakShaver, so you can't email someone outside your organization.

This email was recorded with YakShaver Anywhere, in a project whose destination is Email:

> “Please send an email to Tino. Tino, I’m on the YakShaver docs, on the ‘Tips to create the perfect work item (or email)’ page. First, nice work on the new examples at the top. One small thing: the ‘Quick Reference’ table at the bottom only talks about work items. Could you add a row for emails? No rush, end of this week is fine. So again, please send this as an email to Tino, not a PBI.”

![](/YakShaver/Docs/yakshaver-email-outlook.png)

**✅ Figure: Good example - The email YakShaver sent to Tino, with a clear subject, a link to the video, the request and a screenshot from the video**

**Tip:** Use an email for things that are not a task for the backlog, like a quick question, an FYI or feedback for one person.


## When recording

### 1. State your intent

Begin the recording by clearly stating the purpose. Avoid being vague. Your goal or the options you’re proposing should be as clear as possible.

> *“Please send an email to Tino...”*  
> *“I have a new feature request for the Northwind Project...”*

**❌ Bad example:**
>
> “This is broken.”

**✅ Good example:**
>
> “Create a new bug report for the login page in the Northwind project. The issue is users can’t reset their password. (...)”

Be as specific as possible so the AI knows what action to take and what project you are refering to. 


**Pro Tip:** A good practice is to end your video by restating your intent and the project name. This reinforces your request and helps the AI generate an accurate Work Item.

---

## 2. Give context

Refer to the area of the screen you are discussing. For example, mention the section heading when reviewing a specific section. Explain the user flow as well, such as where you came from and what you are trying to do.

**Note:** Have a look at these [best practices](/docs/giving-context)

**Tip:** When reviewing someone's work, especially if it's a new project or you're not yet familiar with the person, **begin with a positive observation** before suggesting changes. This helps build trust, reinforces good work, and makes feedback easier to receive.

**❌ Bad example:**
>
> “It’s not working here.”

**✅ Good example:**
>
> “On the dashboard, under the ‘Reports’ section, the export button is missing.”


---

## 3. Detail bug reports

For bugs, mention reproduction steps and expected behaviour.

> *“First do this, then do this, then this error occurs, but I expect it to do this”*

**Note:** Have a look at [drafting your bug with enough details](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules/report-bugs-and-suggestions/#tip-1-draft-your-bug-with-enough-details)

**❌ Bad example:**
>
> “Reset password doesn’t work.”

**✅ Good example:**
>
> “Step 1: Go to the login page. Step 2: Enter your email and click ‘Forgot Password’. Step 3: No email is sent. I expect to receive a password reset email.”

---

## 4. Mention additional team members

Say “CC” and their names so you include people who are not in the meeting.

> *“...Cc Adam and Uly”*

**Note:** Creating a PBI or bug report will automatically CC the Product Owner and Tech Lead. [Why?](/docs/user-mention-guidelines-in-work-item-descriptions)

**❌ Bad example:**
>
> “Let someone else know.”

**✅ Good example:**
>
> “Please CC @adam and @uly on this item as they need to review the changes.”

---

## 5. Collaborative effort goes a long way

Inviting a colleague to your YakShaver recording brings fresh perspectives and clearer explanations, and because you must walk them through the workflow beforehand, the recording itself becomes a [refined v2](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules/email-send-a-v2/) with a stronger final result.

If you're the one added to someone else’s yakshave, contribute meaningfully with helpful comments and extra context whenever you can.

![](/YakShaver/Docs/two-people-yakshave-focus.png)

**Figure: Two people in the YakShave call - YakShaves work best when more than one person is involved**

**Tip:** If possible, have another team member present to clarify requirements or add missing details during the recording.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid these common pitfalls to ensure YakShaver produces the best results:

- **Being too vague:** Not stating what you want the AI to do.
  - _example: “This needs fixing.” (What needs fixing? Where?)_
- **Missing context:** Not mentioning which page, feature, or user role is affected.
- **Skipping steps:** Not describing how to reproduce a bug or what the expected outcome is.
- **Not mentioning key people:** Forgetting to CC or mention stakeholders who need to be involved.
- **Recording in a noisy environment:** Background noise can make it hard for the AI to understand your intent.

Take a moment to review your recording and make sure you’ve avoided these mistakes before submitting.

## Sample Video Script

Here’s an example script for a YakShaver recording that follows best practices.

This script clearly states the intent, provides context, details the bug, mentions key people, and demonstrates collaboration.

```text
Hi team, I’d like to create a new bug report for the login page on the project Northwind. 
When users try to reset their password, no email is sent.  

This is how it happened: I went to the login page and entered my email address. Then I clicked on ‘Forgot Password’ link

After 5 minutes monitoring my email inbox I still haven't received my password reset email.  

Please CC @adam and @uly as they need to review this.  
```

## Quick Reference: Do’s and Don’ts

Refer to this table before recording to maximize the quality of your YakShaver work items (and emails)!

| ✅ **Do's** | ❌ **Don’ts** |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Clearly state your intent      | Be vague or ambiguous         |
| Give specific context (page, feature, etc.) | Assume the AI knows what you mean |
| List steps to reproduce bugs   | Skip important details        |
| Mention/CC relevant team members | Forget to involve stakeholders |
| Record in a quiet environment  | Record with lots of background noise |
| Collaborate with others when possible | Work in isolation if clarity is needed |
